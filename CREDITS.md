The tabla's 2D view (`public/media/tabla-from-above.webp`) is a picture rendered from the same model by `scripts/media/render-tabla-picture.mjs`; it carries the same licence, and the artist's name is shown on it.

**The tabla's licence is non-commercial.** Practice Deck is free and uses it on that condition: while the tabla model ships with the app, the app must not be sold, carry advertising or be used in any paid product. To make the app commercial, replace the tabla model first (`models-src/tabla`, `public/models/tabla.*`).

The handpan is built in code (`src/lib/struck/handpanModel.js`) and has no model to credit. Its look follows the D Aegean pans of [Ayasa Instruments](https://ayasainstruments.com/), used as a visual reference only: none of their pictures or files are in this repository.

"Re-encoded" means the textures were resized and saved as WebP and the model was turned and scaled into the stage's units by `scripts/models/prepare.mjs`; on the six whose strings the stage draws, the parts were also split and renamed. On the two drum kits, the pieces of each drum were gathered into a part of its own, so that it can move and light when it is hit. The second cello was written with an older kind of material, which was converted to the kind the stage draws: its colours and pictures are kept, its gloss is approximated. The originals are not stored in this repository: `models-src/` is ignored. Each prepared file in `public/models/` also carries its credit line in its glTF `asset.copyright` field.

The credit list lives in `src/lib/stage/modelCredits.js`. The Help dialog and the preparation script both read it.

The bass, antique cello and electronic kit also have their surface geometry simplified within each material and playable part. The measured strings, frets and bridge are kept.
